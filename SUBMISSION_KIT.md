## Written answers (drafts)

Rewrite these in your own voice. Anything in [brackets] is yours to fill in; do not make up personal details.

**1. What is the title of your app?**
SignSpark

**2. Explain the app's purpose.**
SignSpark teaches American Sign Language to beginners for free. It covers the alphabet, numbers and 59 everyday signs in 35 short lessons, with spaced-repetition flashcards, a word of the day, practice games and lessons on ASL grammar and Deaf culture. Each sign is described the way ASL teachers describe signs: handshape, location, movement, palm direction and facial expression. The goal is to help hearing people take the first step toward communicating with the Deaf community, and to point them toward learning from Deaf signers.

**3. What inspired you to create this app?**
[Your real reason, in 3 to 5 sentences. Questions that might help: Do you know someone who is Deaf or hard of hearing? Was there a moment when you could not communicate with someone? Have you tried other language apps and noticed ASL was missing or hard to practice?]

**4. What technical/coding difficulty did you face, and how did you address it?**
The hardest problem was showing signs without photos or videos. Images would need permission to use and would make the app heavy, so instead each of the 45 handshapes is a small recipe: which fingers are straight, curled or bent, the angle of each finger, and where the thumb goes. Code turns each recipe into an SVG drawing using trigonometry for finger angles. Letters like M, N and T look almost the same and differ only in where the thumb tucks in, so the drawing order of each finger had to be controlled to show the thumb tip between exactly the right fingers. The same system mirrors every drawing for left-handed signers with one line of CSS. Another challenge was keeping day streaks correct across time zones and daylight-saving changes, which is covered by unit tests. [Edit this to match what you worked on and understand.]

**5. What did you learn while participating in the CAC? What was your biggest takeaway?**
[Your own answer. Possible directions, if true: how spaced repetition works; how much of ASL is in the face and body, not just the hands; how to plan a project and test it; how to direct and check an AI tool's work.]

**6. What would you change about your app if you were to create a 2.0 version?**
I would add camera practice, using in-browser hand tracking (such as Google's MediaPipe) to check a learner's handshape and give instant feedback. I would also work with Deaf signers to record short videos of every sign, add more units (school, emotions, time, colors), and add a classroom mode so teachers can assign lessons and see progress.
